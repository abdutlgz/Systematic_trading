Acquirers Overpay, Markets Learn Slowly: A Market-Neutral 8-K Event Strategy Found by a Pre-Registered, Multiple-Testing-Controlled Search

Gator Quant Hacks 2026 · Systematic Trading Track · Team: [team member names] · Code: [public GitHub URL] · Reproduce: python event8k/grid.py --oos, python event8k/acq_analysis.py

1. Summary

When a US company worth $2B or more files an 8-K announcing an acquisition agreement (Massive 8-K disclosure category), we short its stock at the next close, hedge market beta with SPY, and hold for 60 trading days, at 2% of capital per event. The rule was selected blindly from a pre-registered grid of 165 event × holding × size configurations, using in-sample data only (Jan 2022–Sep 2025). Its single out-of-sample run (Oct 2025–Sep 2026) confirmed it. Net of 10 bps per side and a 1%/yr borrow fee: in-sample Sharpe 0.98 (8.3%/yr, max drawdown −7.6%), out-of-sample Sharpe 0.82 (9.9%/yr, −12.2%), with beta to SPY of −0.02. Every calendar year from 2022 to 2026 is positive.

What we claim, and what we do not. Over the full 4.7 years, t = 2.01; shorting the same stocks on random dates never beat it (0 of 100 placebos); and it survives doubled costs plus a 3% borrow fee. But it was chosen from 165 cells and does not survive Benjamini-Hochberg-Yekutieli or Holm correction in-sample. Part of its return is the 2022–26 large-cap premium (SMB loading −0.49; factor-adjusted alpha +3.2%/yr, t = 0.93). It is the most statistically supported of the 32 hypotheses and 165 grid cells we tested, all reported, and it agrees with a 40-year literature on acquirer underperformance. It is not proven alpha.

2. Economic Hypothesis

Why acquirers drift down. Bidders systematically overpay. Management hubris and the winner's curse in competitive auctions (Roll 1986), empire building, and optimistic synergy forecasts mean that the average large deal destroys acquirer value (Moeller, Schlingemann & Stulz 2005). The market reacts to the announcement, but only partly: acquirers keep underperforming for months to years afterwards (Loughran & Vijh 1997; Savor & Lu 2009 for stock-financed deals), as integration costs, financing and diluted guidance arrive gradually. Who is on the other side: holders anchored to management's synergy story, analysts who revise slowly, and index and passive owners who keep the stock regardless. Why it persists: each deal is idiosyncratic and hard to value in real time, shorting is costly and squeeze-prone, and the information arrives as soft text (8-K exhibits), not numbers. Testable predictions (checked in Section 5): the drift comes from acquirers, not deal targets; it is event-specific (not a property of these stocks on other dates); it builds over weeks, not on day 1; it is not a market-beta bet.

How we found it, and why that matters. We did not start here. After 32 pre-registered hypotheses (Section 5.4), we committed a grid to git before running it (event8k/GRID_PLAN.md): 34 event definitions × {20, 60, 120} sessions × {≥$2B, ≥$10B}, with a fixed winner rule (best cell surviving BHY; if none, the highest deflated Sharpe). No cell survived BHY. The fallback winner was this one. The hypothesis above explains a pattern found by search, which we disclose prominently, and the out-of-sample test is the evidence that matters.

3. Data and Universe

Events: Massive Filings & Disclosures API (sponsor data), 8-K category acquisition_agreement, all US filers, 2022-01-01 to 2026-09-30 (taxonomy coverage begins in 2022), with one event per filer per 30 days. Universe: filer market cap ≥ $2B on the day before filing (Massive point-in-time reference data), giving 1,109 events across 705 tickers. Firms later acquired or delisted remain in the sample; the 3.7% of events with no point-in-time cap are excluded. Prices: Massive daily bars, split- and dividend-adjusted; SPY is the hedge. Access: Massive REST API with the hackathon key: /stocks/filings/8-K/vX/disclosures (events), /v3/reference/tickers/{t}?date= (point-in-time cap), /v2/aggs (bars). About 60k requests are cached on disk; the raw data is licensed and not committed, and event8k/build_grid.py rebuilds it. Attribution only: Kenneth French 5 factors + momentum; CBOE VIX. Split: 4.75 years of data, so the track's 20% rule binds (0.95 years < 2 years). In-sample is 2022-01-01 to 2025-09-30; out-of-sample is 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30, run once for the winner. Disclosure: an earlier, different 8-K strategy (abrupt CFO exits) was evaluated once on the same OOS window; the winner was chosen without looking at it. Missing data: no forward-filling from later data; a stock that stops printing is carried at its last close.

4. Methodology

  • Timing: 8-K filing dates carry no time of day and can be after the close, so we enter at the close of the first session strictly after the filing date. No same-day trading.
  • Position: short 2% of capital per event; long SPY × β (β from sessions −260 to −11, clipped to [0.5, 1.5]); hold 60 sessions, exit at the close. Gross short is capped at 100% of capital: new events are skipped while the book is full (13% of IS entries, 33% OOS).
  • Costs: 10 bps per side on stocks (half-spread plus slippage for ≥$2B names) and 2 bps on SPY; borrow fee 1%/yr on short notional (general collateral for liquid large and mid-caps). Stress cases: 2× costs and 3% borrow. Sharpe is computed on excess returns, with T-bill interest on cash and the short rebate excluded.
  • Selection statistics: two-sided p-values on in-sample Sharpe t-stats across all 165 valid cells; BHY FDR control (valid under dependence); Holm; deflated Sharpe (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) with N = 165. The direction (long/short) was set by the in-sample sign, which the two-sided tests account for.
  • Evidence beyond Sharpe: a random-date placebo on the same tickers; a P&L decomposition into stock and hedge legs; execution-lag tests; neighbouring holds and sizes; Fama-French 5 + momentum regression (Newey-West errors).

5.1 Is it the event, or something else?

  • Random-date placebo: shorting the same stocks (behind the 732 in-sample events) on random dates (≥90 days from their deals), with identical sizing and hedging, gives mean Sharpe −0.53 (95th percentile −0.13). 0 of 100 placebos reach 0.98. On ordinary dates these stocks beat their hedge; after an acquisition filing they lag it.
  • Acquirers, not targets: per event over 60 sessions, the stock returns −0.07% while its β-hedge earns +2.61%. Likely deal targets (post-filing volatility below half the pre-filing level, i.e. pinned to the deal price) are only 43 events (+0.85%); the other 689, mostly acquirers, earn +2.79% each. This is the predicted source.
  • Not beta, not timing: β to SPY is −0.01 IS and −0.14 OOS; mean P&L on days SPY moves ±2% is about 0. Entering 1 or 5 days late changes little (0.92, 1.12): the drift is slow, there is no same-day lookahead, and there is room to execute.
  • Neighbours (IS Sharpe): ≥$2B: 20d 0.62, 60d 0.98, 120d 0.88; ≥$10B: 0.36, 0.41, 0.83. All six are positive (a plateau, not a spike); the effect is weaker in mega-caps, which are more efficiently priced.
  • Factor attribution (full period): market β ≈ 0.01, SMB −0.49, R² 0.44, alpha +3.2%/yr (t = 0.93). The book is short mid/large caps against cap-weighted SPY, so the 2022–26 large-cap premium explains a meaningful share. The placebo, which carries the same size exposure and loses, says the post-filing window adds to it, but we cannot reject that the factor-adjusted alpha is zero.

5.2 Multiple testing, stated plainly

In-sample, 165 cells had at least 40 events; none survives BHY at 5% or Holm; the winner's p = 0.058 and deflated Sharpe probability 0.67. The second-best cells were also shorts after negative or complex corporate events (internal-control weakness, CEO+CFO joint exits, restatements, auditor dismissal), which is consistent with slow incorporation of bad 8-K news but individually insignificant. The out-of-sample year (0.82, 303 new events) is what lifts this above a search artefact; one year is still short (95% CI on OOS Sharpe ≈ ±2).

5.3 Strategy 2: cross-asset momentum (diversifier)

Our first, fully pre-registered strategy (rules committed before any data, commit 6b6764d): 12-month time-series momentum on 10 ETFs, with volatility-scaled positions, monthly rebalancing and a VIX overlay. Net of costs and financing: IS 2008–Sep 2024 Sharpe 0.29, OOS Oct 2024–Sep 2026 Sharpe 0.72; correlation with AQR's TSMOM factor 0.72; correlation with the acquirer short −0.10. It is a credible but low-Sharpe trend premium (in-sample t ≈ 1.2) whose OOS gain was mostly equity beta (0.50). Details are in Appendix B and strategy/.

6. Risk Management

  • Limits (implemented): 2% of capital per name, so a single name can lose at most about 2% × its 60-day rise. Gross short ≤ 100%, with the SPY hedge sized to β; net market exposure is about zero by construction. On average 43 names are held (mean gross 86% IS, 97% OOS), which is well diversified across sectors.
  • Squeeze risk (the main tail): the worst positions were IonQ (+83% in 60 days, −1.7% of capital), Rocket Lab (+68%, −1.2%) and QXO (+59%, −1.1%): speculative high-momentum acquirers. Daily VaR95 is 0.97%, ES99 1.78%, worst day −2.4%, skew −0.16, excess kurtosis 0.7: no hidden short-volatility profile.
  • Regimes: Sharpe 0.65 when VIX > 25 vs 0.97 otherwise; every calendar year positive; ex-best-year (2024) Sharpe 0.86. The OOS drawdown (−12.2%, early 2026) came in a risk-on rally in speculative stocks.
  • Factor exposure: SMB −0.49. A size-neutral hedge (an equal-weight or mid-cap ETF instead of SPY) is the obvious refinement, but it would be a new trial and is untested here.
  • Proposed live rules (not backtested, so they would be new trials): a per-name stop-out at +40% against the short; skip names in the top decile of 12-month momentum (squeeze-prone); halve gross if the strategy is down 10% from peak; monitor borrow fees daily and drop names above 3%.

Against an 8–10% net return, impact takes a third of the edge near $100M and half near $250M, so the realistic capacity is about $100–150M. Larger books should scale position size to ADV (for example, cap each trade at 5% of ADV and work entries over 2–3 days; the lag test shows a 5-day delay costs nothing). About 190–200 entries per year, roughly 4 trades a week. Shorts in ≥$2B names are generally easy to borrow, and the 3%-borrow stress keeps Sharpe at 0.58–0.68. A margin/prime account is required for the short book plus the SPY hedge (gross about 1.7× at full deployment).

8. Limitations and Next Steps

  • Selection from a search: no cell survived multiple-testing correction; the evidence rests on one OOS year, the placebo and the literature. A second OOS year is the real test. Our live positions are hash-stamped in FORWARD_LEDGER*.md.
  • Size factor: factor-adjusted alpha is not significant. Next: a size-matched hedge and a split between cash and stock deals (Savor & Lu predict stock-financed deals drive the effect).
  • Short history: 8-K categories exist only from 2022. Extending with SEC EDGAR Item 1.01/2.01 parsing back to 2004 would allow decades of testing.
  • Classification: acquirer vs target is inferred from volatility, and Massive tags are model-assigned. Hand-checking a sample is next.
