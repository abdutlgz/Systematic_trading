1. Summary

We trade ten liquid ETFs (equities, Treasuries, gold, commodities, the US dollar and REITs) in the direction of each asset's own 12-month return, sized to equal volatility, rebalanced monthly, and executed one day after the signal at 10 bps per side. An extension cuts exposure in half when the VIX closes above 30. Every rule was committed to git (commit 6b6764d) before any price was downloaded. Net of costs, the in-sample (2008–Sep 2024) Sharpe is 0.26 (max drawdown −28%). The single out-of-sample run (Oct 2024–Sep 2026) gave 0.90 (+11.2%/yr, max drawdown −12.6%), and 0.85 at doubled costs.

We do not claim a proven edge. The in-sample t-statistic is 1.05, the out-of-sample Sharpe's 95% interval is [−0.48, +2.35], and out-of-sample returns are explained by a 0.50 equity beta (alpha t = −0.07). The strategy is a cheap, transparent, diversifying trend premium with expected Sharpe near its long-run 0.25, not 0.90. Our main contribution is the testing: 27 pre-registered trials across five research lines, a nine-pitfall statistical audit, and full disclosure of what failed (Section 5.3).

2. Economic Hypothesis (written before results)

Edge and counterparty. Prices under-react to new information and then trend as it diffuses, and late investors chase the move (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012; Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen 2017). The other side is hedgers and slow-moving capital who trade regardless of trend: central banks and corporates in FX, producers hedging commodities, and investors rebalancing to fixed weights. Why it persists: it is part behavioral bias and part risk premium. Trend followers lose in choppy, reversing markets, which limits arbitrage capital.

Our extension. Momentum losses cluster after volatility spikes, when trends reverse sharply (Daniel & Moskowitz 2016; Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015). We predicted that halving exposure when the VIX is above 30 lowers drawdowns without destroying the premium. Testable predictions: (1) positive net Sharpe in- and out-of-sample; (2) a plateau: 3/6/9-month lookbacks also positive; (3) the overlay lowers max drawdown. It fails if out-of-sample Sharpe ≤ 0 or only the 12-month lookback works.

3. Data and Universe

Instruments: SPY, EFA, EEM, IWM (equity); TLT, IEF (Treasuries); GLD, DBC (commodities); UUP (USD); VNQ (REITs). All are broad index ETFs listed by March 2007; the signal waits for 252 days of history. Prices: Yahoo Finance daily closes, split- and dividend-adjusted (total return), 2007-01-03 to 2026-09-30. VIX: CBOE close via Yahoo ^VIX (identical to FRED VIXCLS, whose endpoint timed out); a market close, never revised. Attribution only: Kenneth French daily 5 factors + momentum. Split: 18.75 years of data; 20% would be 3.75 years, so the 2-year cap binds. IS 2008-01-02 to 2024-09-30 (16.75 yrs); OOS 2024-10-01 to 2026-09-30, run once. Data issues: missing days are dropped, never forward-filled from later data; the VIX uses its last known close. Survivorship: no single stocks, but the ETF menu was chosen in 2026 with knowledge that these funds became large. We disclose this as residual bias.

4. Methodology

  • Signal (month-end close t): sign of the trailing 252-day total return of each ETF.
  • Sizing: each asset targets 10%/√N annualized volatility using a 60-day EWMA estimate known at t; per-asset weight capped at ±0.5; gross leverage capped at 2.0×; cash earns 0% (conservative).
  • Execution: weights decided at close t are traded at close t+1. Holdings drift with prices between monthly rebalances, and turnover is measured against drifted holdings.
  • Costs: 5 bps commission/spread + 5 bps slippage = 10 bps per side. These ETFs' quoted spreads are about 1–3 bps and Webull charges no commission, so 10 bps is conservative. All results are also shown at 20 bps.
  • Overlay: all weights × 0.5 if the VIX close at t > 30 (threshold fixed a priori, never tuned).
  • Pre-declared trials: base 12m, base + overlay (primary), lookbacks 3/6/9m (in-sample only), 2× costs. Engine: our own event-driven pandas simulator, cross-checked against a vectorized version (identical numbers).
  • Evidence beyond Sharpe: Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014), a 200-run random-sign placebo, execution-lag sensitivity, probability of backtest overfitting via CSCV (Bailey et al. 2017), multiple-testing corrections (Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016), and factor regressions with Newey-West errors.

5.1 How much of this is real?

  • Luck: Deflated Sharpe probability 0.80 (N = 5 momentum trials) and 0.76 (N = 20). Only 0.5% of 200 random-sign placebos with identical sizing and costs beat it (placebo mean −0.27, 95th percentile 0.10).
  • Lookahead: same-bar execution (a deliberate bug) adds only +0.05 Sharpe (0.31). Lags of 2 and 5 days give 0.21 and 0.25. The decay is smooth, with no leak.
  • Costs: the break-even cost is 54 bps per side, 5.4× our assumption.
  • Factor attribution: in-sample alpha is +1.6%/yr (t = 0.64), loading on the equity momentum factor (β = 0.35, t = 16). Out-of-sample alpha is ≈ 0 (t = −0.07) with market β = 0.50 (t = 12.5), R² = 0.60. The OOS gain is trend-following into a rising equity market, not new alpha.
  • Sample length: two years of out-of-sample data cannot separate 0.25 from 0.90. The OOS Sharpe 95% interval is [−0.48, +2.35]. We set expectations at the long-run in-sample level.

5.2 Did the extension work?

Partly. In-sample, the overlay cut max drawdown from −29.4% to −28.3% at the same Sharpe. Out-of-sample it never triggered: the VIX was above 30 on 14 days and peaked at 52.3 on 8 April 2025, but no month-end close exceeded 25.2. A month-end check misses short volatility spikes. This is a design lesson; we did not change the rule after seeing it (Section 8).

No screened idea met our pre-registered promotion rule. The Deflated Sharpe probability flagged the two best (0.84 and 0.71 < 0.90), and both then failed out-of-sample: the statistics did their job. Overnight drift, weekly reversal and sector momentum break even below realistic costs.

6. Risk Management

  • Limits (implemented): per-asset volatility targeting; |weight| ≤ 0.5 per ETF, so a 10% gap in one asset costs at most 5% of capital; gross ≤ 2.0×; net exposure is unconstrained by design (trend can be net short). With 10 assets across 6 asset classes, no sector limit is needed.
  • De-risking (implemented): the VIX > 30 half-exposure rule; the monthly rebalance resets volatility weights as realized volatility rises.
  • Factor exposure: long-run market β = 0.08 in-sample, rising to 0.50 out-of-sample; equity momentum β = 0.35. Investors should treat this as a trend/momentum sleeve, not market-neutral alpha.
  • Tail and regime: worst month −10.4% in-sample (−7.2% OOS); daily skew −0.11, excess kurtosis 7.1. Sharpe is 0.46 when the prior-day VIX ≤ 25 and −0.07 when it is above 25, so this sample shows no crisis alpha. 2008 (+17.9%) and 2022 (+9.7%) were good years; 2018 (−15.7%), 2016 (−12.3%) and 2023 (−8.9%) were the worst. The best year contributes only 16% of gains, and Sharpe excluding it is still 0.26.
  • Proposed live rules (not backtested, so they would be new trials): check the VIX trigger daily, not monthly; halve gross exposure after a −20% drawdown from peak and restore it after a new 3-month high; stop trading if realized volatility exceeds 2× target for 10 days. These must be validated on new data before use.

Capacity is set by the two thin funds, UUP (USD, ADV ≈ $33M) and DBC (ADV ≈ $28M); SPY, TLT and EFA could absorb hundreds of millions. Replacing UUP/DBC with currency and commodity futures would raise capacity by an order of magnitude; that is our first next step. Turnover of 7.3×/yr is low, so the fixed-cost drag is about 0.7%/yr.

8. Limitations and Next Steps

  • Short OOS, weak significance: no result here is significant after accounting for 27 trials. At a true Sharpe of 0.3, reaching t = 2 takes about 45 years of data; we have 18.75.
  • Hindsight in the menu: the ten ETFs were picked in 2026. A broader futures universe (Databento, 1980s onward) would remove this and lengthen the sample.
  • Overlay design flaw: month-end checks miss short volatility spikes. A daily trigger is pre-registered as future work and will be tested only on data after 2026-10-03.
  • Simplifications: 0% yield on idle cash (understates returns when rates are positive); closing-price fills; Yahoo data quality not audited against a second vendor.
  • Next: futures universe for capacity; combine with the 8-K finding that options under-price moves after executive departures (bonus note) as a separate long-volatility sleeve; paper-trade via the Webull starter before any capital.
