# Combined project review — October 3, 2026

Recommendation: keep cross-asset momentum as the main research entry, present Massive as a separate, honest options/event study, and develop Gemini as an evidence-review application. The three components currently share context and data; their combination has not demonstrated better investment outcomes. More strategies or larger models are not the highest-value next step.

## Competition assessment

The supplied main-track rubric scores the strategy, not the number of integrations. My current reviewer estimate is **28/50**, down one evidence point from the earlier 29/50 comparison because this audit found a metric-labeling issue and a cost-accounting inconsistency. This is not an official judge score.

Under the user's criterion-5 cap, evidence cannot exceed 4 if either judge reproduction or the required absence of lookahead/OOS tuning fails. That would reduce this estimate to **26/50**. Neither box can be certified merely because local tests pass. Historical returns being positive is not proof of alpha and is not the scoring objective.

The Massive notebook uses a DIFFERENT rubric: novelty30, rigor30, sealed replication20, realism10, communication10. Do not combine it with the main 50-point score. The original executive-exit null study is more submission-ready than the new momentum bridge. The bridge only accepts cached `is/oos` fixtures, omits expiry results and intervals in its conditional summaries, and has not run a judge's arbitrary window. It cannot claim sealed replication credit. The older general runner takes dates but its ordinary `post` calculation enters at filing-day close before the optional acceptance-time section; the safer new pilot's execution rule has not been integrated into that submission runner.

The [MLH Gemini partner page](https://www.mlh.com/partners/gemini) promotes Gemini projects, but supplies no event-specific judging rubric. I cannot assign an official Gemini-track score from that page. The project demonstrates real API use and inspectable outputs; it lacks independent accuracy evidence, arbitrary new-document input and a demonstrated benefit over a keyword baseline.

## Verified results and material findings

**Main calculation reproduces.** Recomputing the frozen VIX momentum variant matches the saved 4,716-session return series with maximum absolute error about 1e-16. This is an exact cache comparison, not a fresh provider download or a full cash-account audit. All three new offline suites (`strategy/massive_bridge/checks.py`, `strategy_8k/momentum_bridge/checks.py`, `gemini_track/checks.py`) pass. No new paid inference, new event-window acquisition, original strategy change or sealed-window access was performed for this review.

**Sharpe needs correction or relabeling.** `backtest.stats` and the financed headline tables use mean TOTAL return / return standard deviation. Financing costs and cash interest have already been added, but the risk-free benchmark has not been subtracted. The conventional cash-benchmark Sharpe uses differential returns; see [Sharpe's original definition](https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/sr/SR.htm).

| Financed strategy measurement | IS 2008–Sep 2024 | Historical test Oct 2024–Sep 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Saved institutional total-return / volatility ratio | 0.287 | 0.720 |
| Cash-excess Sharpe, existing RF forward-fill convention | 0.203 | **0.371** |
| Cash-excess Sharpe, retail financing | 0.058 | **0.120** |

The cached French RF series ends August 31, 2026. The full-window excess numbers above use the existing prior-value forward-fill for September; they are estimates, not verified September RF observations. Restricting the historical test to dates with observed RF, October 2024–August 2026, gives institutional 0.478 and retail 0.229 over 480 sessions. Do not compare these different windows as a model improvement. Annual returns and saved P&L were not changed by this review.

**There is a reproducible transaction-cost unit error.** `simulate` drifts holdings after the day's P&L, then measures turnover in units of that updated NAV, but subtracts `turnover * cost_rate` directly from a return measured on previous NAV. On a day when a full invested position rises 10% and is liquidated at 10 bps, code reports 9.900% rather than 9.890%: sale proceeds are 1.10 × previous NAV, so the cost is 0.110%, not 0.100%. This is a one-basis-point error in that synthetic liquidation, not an estimate of the historical aggregate error. Rebuild the dollar cash/position ledger and reconcile financing within it; measure the historical impact before updating notes. Target-weight treatment of costs should be explicitly defined. This is a bookkeeping correction, not a reason to retune signals or label the reviewed historical period fresh OOS.

**Risk descriptions overstate implemented limits.** Whole-history gross reaches 2.232x and one ETF reaches 0.50590 weight on four sessions. In the historical test, gross reaches 2.119x and exceeds 2x on 180/501 sessions (35.9%). Thus a 10% one-asset gap is not universally limited to 5% NAV. Inverse-volatility sizing also does not equalize marginal portfolio risk when assets are correlated. The VIX overlay has zero test-window month-end triggers; the maximum month-end VIX is 25.25. A daily monitoring/cap rule would be a new specification needing its own evaluation.

**The added 8-K link is not validated.** Guidance-withdrawal acquisition found 41 records including warm-up, zero in the sponsor universe, and zero changed targets. Executive-exit protective puts reduced marked downside tails, but their 21-session quarter-matched event advantage reversed from +0.58% in 2024–25 to −0.55% in 2026. The 2026 primary cell has 29 events and three matched quarters, below its fixed promotion gate. Every event and control passes the positive SPY filter: there is no contrasting regime and no identified momentum interaction. The study conditions on a single equity sleeve's sign, not a demonstrated interaction with the complete cross-asset portfolio.

**The options evidence has execution and selection limits.** The safer pilot requires same-session positive-volume marks, and records 1,518 missing-mark exclusions in the 2026 loop (these are attempted horizon/OTM observations, not 1,518 separate events). Report retention by issuer, quarter, horizon and leg, and compare retained versus rejected events. Synthetic spot from American-option parity, assumed premium costs, and imperfect issuer composition in ordinary-day controls prevent quote-executable claims. Current historical category snapshots do not establish vendor-label availability on historical dates. Original same-day entry and causal claims about a filing-day surprise need acceptance and prior-announcement timestamps; a calendar-day association is not evidence that the filing caused the move.

**Gemini is a real but small demonstration.** Eight actual `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` responses used 2,714 prompt and 911 candidate-output tokens. All eight passed quotation checks and matched author labels. Keywords matched all eight too. Inputs were only 107–429-character excerpts, selected chronologically rather than by outcomes; this is not an independent full-filing benchmark. A diagnostic intentionally labeled “Jane will retire” as resignation while quoting that exact sentence: `validate()` returned no errors. That is consistent with its documented provenance-only role, but demonstrates why “8/8 validated” must not imply semantic accuracy. The UI should distinguish source matching, semantic assessment and information missing from the excerpt.

**Reproduction and packaging are incomplete for the combined project.** New directories are untracked in the working tree; no clean-checkout reproduction was performed. Gemini and the conditional options pilot depend on ignored pickle fixtures and caches. Older pandas in the main environment cannot read the sponsor's newer pickle objects. New code reads notebook functions by fixed cell numbers, so unrelated notebook edits can break it. Main `requirements.txt` omits `requests`, which the new ETF bridge imports. Gemini authentication only reads the local secret file, rather than the usual environment-key path, and its newest-eight fixture assertion prevents new-window input. Responses are cached by text hash alone; changes to prompt/model/schema/generation configuration should produce an explicit compatible cache identity rather than an assertion during rendering.

**Submission prose is inconsistent.** README claims 27 trials, quant-note title 31, note/FAQ 32, while later research adds additional hypotheses and checks. Reproduction reruns and sensitivity cells must be distinguished from independent hypotheses in one ledger. The FAQ's claims about first access to OOS and all research being pre-registered are stronger than the combined repository supports. Frozen specifications should remain unchanged; add dated corrections to the submission-facing note and regenerate its PDF. The new Gemini demo has had structural checks but no browser visual QA in this environment.

## Three highest-value improvements

**1. Make one judge-ready, internally consistent release.** Implement a dollar-level accounting correction; report cash-excess Sharpe beside returns and factor alpha; label the exact financing/RF assumptions. Add one versioned setup and reproduce command, portable input manifests, environment-based keys, and a date-parameterized Massive runner using the conservative delayed-entry rule. Verify in a fresh environment without private caches. Use one trial ledger and reconcile README, notes, FAQ, HTML and PDFs. Keep original protocols and clearly mark corrections and retrospective tests. This protects evidence points and the cap rule more directly than another backtest search.

**2. Give Gemini a task where language understanding can add measured value.** Accept a new filing or text input, retrieve the full relevant original section, handle multiple executives, past versus future events, negated statements and missing details, and expose evidence spans and uncertainty. Freeze a prompt, build a 30–50-document label set not used for prompt editing, and have another human adjudicate labels independently. Compare field accuracy, abstention, unsupported-claim rate, latency, token cost and time saved against keyword rules. Cache model/prompt/schema/source together and include error states. A useful question is “which transitions retain continuity and which remain unclear?”, not “will this stock go up?”. This is the strongest Gemini-track improvement.

**3. Validate an actual risk workflow prospectively.** Add margin/cash/borrow monitoring, executable quote/volume checks, agreed gross/exposure limits and a documented response when data are absent. For the 8-K interaction, timestamp label arrival and original public announcements; keep the rule fixed and collect both positive and negative momentum regimes. Match controls by issuer and calendar period and evaluate protection cost versus tail-loss reduction with all rejected observations disclosed. No new data means no claim that daily VIX, an AI filter or an options sleeve improves the strategy. Existing years can support exploratory diagnostics, not recreate an untouched holdout.

Treat a 32–35/50 future main-track score as a conditional aspiration if judge-ready reproducibility, stronger controls and genuinely new evidence are delivered, not a forecast. Gemini product improvements do not automatically raise the strategy's innovation score. The current project is strongest as transparent research plus an evidence-review tool; presenting it as one proven AI-enhanced trading strategy would weaken its argument.
