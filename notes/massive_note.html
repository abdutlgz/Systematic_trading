1. Hypotheses (each committed to git before any option price for it was requested)

  • H-A, executive exits → cash-secured put (ceo/cfo/executive_officer_departure, pooled; commit dbbfe6c). A vivid, ambiguous exit makes holders overpay for downside puts (demand pressure, Gârleanu et al. 2009), while mega-cap businesses rarely change overnight. So the put seller earns an event premium beyond the ordinary variance risk premium.
  • H-B, CFO appointment → cash-secured put (workshop suggestion; HYPOTHESIS_5.md). Filling the CFO seat resolves financial-reporting uncertainty, but implied volatility mean-reverts with a lag, so sold puts collect the leftover premium. Pre-declared side tags: CEO appointment, and CFO departure (predicted no edge).

Spec = the notebook's defaults, so it matches what judges run: top-100 universe, 3–6m expiry bucket, entry at the close of the filing session, 5% OTM, fixed horizons, and the notebook's placebo (same tickers on ordinary days, 30 days or more from any event). The starter's API cell and pipeline cells run unchanged; we only set the window and the event table (exec_exits_8k.ipynb, EVENTS/START/END).

2. Method: three windows and a star rule fixed in advance

Options history reaches back to 2022, so we use three non-overlapping windows. For H-B: discovery 2022–23, confirmation 2024–25, final Jan–Aug 2026, run once. A cell is "starred" when the 95% bootstrap CI of events minus placebo excludes 0. H-B passes discovery only if the CSP is positive and starred at ≥2 of the 10/21/42-session/expiry horizons. For H-A (registered earlier): in-sample 2024–25, OOS Jan–Aug 2026 once. Trial 9 (long straddle after exits, HYPOTHESIS_9.md) was tested once on 2022–23, a window never used for it. In total, 9 pre-registered trials are reported (TRIALS.md).

H-A fails. The put trade is about zero in 2024–25. In 2026 it turns positive only because exit stocks rallied +6.8–9.0% vs placebo, and a short put is long delta. That is regime luck, not the volatility premium. H-B fails. It has no stars in discovery. The nearly-starred expiry cell (+2.34%) was registered as a follow-up (trial 8, HYPOTHESIS_8.md) and tested on unseen 2024–25: −0.23%. The near-star was noise.

4. What replicated, and why it still cannot be traded

Figure. |Realized| ÷ implied move (3–6m ATM straddle on the pre-event session, √t-scaled), mean and 95% CI, in three windows.

In all three windows, the filing-day move is under-priced: the 1-session ratio is 1.13 vs placebo 0.88 (2022–23), 1.13 vs 0.82 (2024–25), and 1.16 vs 0.91 (2026). The multi-week excess is not robust: at 10–21 sessions it is 1.05–1.43 in 2024–26, but 0.91 vs 1.07–1.12 in 2022–23 (reversed). So the market under-prices the exit surprise, then reprices within a day. A buyer entering at the filing-session close (the first tradeable moment) is too late. Trial 9 confirms this: the post-filing straddle loses −1.15% vs placebo at 21 sessions, with a 31% hit rate. Selling puts does not pay either (0 of 54 starred CSP variants in the pre-declared sensitivity grid). Information in leadership 8-Ks is priced quickly: an honest, coherent null.

5. What would break it, how we would trade it, and our sealed-window predictions

Limits: small samples (14–163 priced events per window); a static 2026 top-100 list (survivorship); filing date ≠ announcement time (many exits are press-released first); daily last-trade marks, not NBBO mid. What could still work (untested): capturing the day-0 surprise needs entry before the market reprices. That means using EDGAR acceptance timestamps to trade the open after an overnight filing, or intraday data. Top-100 ATM options trade almost every session; at 1–2 contracts per event, ADV does not bind. Hash-stamped predictions (FORWARD_LEDGER.md P1–P4, FORWARD_LEDGER_2.md P5–P7): on your window, exit and CFO-appointment CSPs will not beat placebo, and stars will appear at about the chance rate. Our P2 (exit realized/implied > placebo at 21 sessions) already failed on 2022–23, and we report that against ourselves.
